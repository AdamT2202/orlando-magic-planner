Animal Kingdom for Families with young kids in Winter

Everything you need to plan the perfect Animal Kingdom trip — rides, dining, crowd tips and more.

What to expect

Visiting Animal Kingdom with young kids in winter brings a magical combination of cooler, comfortable temperatures and festive holiday atmosphere that makes exploring the park an absolute delight. December through February typically sees moderate crowd levels, with a peak around the holiday season in late December before tapering off into quieter January and February visits. The lush theming of the park feels especially enchanting in winter, and mild Florida weather averaging 60–75°F means little ones stay comfortable throughout the day without overheating.

Top attractions for Families with young kids

  • Festival of the Lion King – This spectacular live show features vibrant costumes, acrobatics, and beloved songs that captivate toddlers and young children from start to finish.
  • Kilimanjaro Safaris – Young kids are thrilled to spot real elephants, giraffes, and lions up close on this gentle open-vehicle adventure through the African savanna.
  • The Boneyard – A massive dinosaur-themed outdoor play area where little ones can dig for fossils, climb, and burn off energy in a completely safe environment.
  • Finding Nemo: The Big Blue…and Beyond! – A colorful, musical theater show featuring beloved characters that keeps young audiences engaged and entertained throughout.
  • Na'vi River Journey – This smooth, visually stunning boat ride through Pandora is gentle enough for toddlers and offers breathtaking bioluminescent scenery that sparks young imaginations.

Dining tips

Restaurantosaurus in DinoLand offers familiar kid-friendly comfort foods like burgers, chicken nuggets, and mac and cheese, making it an easy win for even the pickiest young eaters. Flame Tree Barbecue is an excellent quick-service option with outdoor seating overlooking a scenic waterway, perfect for families who want to keep moving without sacrificing a satisfying meal. For a special sit-down experience, Tusker House Restaurant offers a character dining buffet where kids can meet Donald Duck and friends in safari attire, creating unforgettable memories. Consider booking dining reservations well in advance for table-service restaurants, especially around the busy holiday period in December.

Winter planning tips

  • Arrive at rope drop – Getting to the park at opening allows families with young kids to experience the most popular attractions before crowds build mid-morning.
  • Pack layers for early mornings – Winter mornings in Orlando can dip into the low 60s, so bring light jackets or sweaters that can be stowed in a bag as temperatures rise.
  • Book Lightning Lane passes strategically – Use Lightning Lane for Na'vi River Journey and Kilimanjaro Safaris to avoid long waits that can frustrate tired young children.
  • Plan around nap schedules – Schedule a mid-afternoon break at your resort hotel to rest young children, then return to the park in the early evening for lower crowds and cooler temperatures.
  • Take advantage of January and February – If holiday travel is flexible, visiting in January or February offers significantly shorter wait times and a more relaxed pace ideal for families with toddlers.

How to plan your day

Start your morning at rope drop by heading straight to Pandora – The World of Avatar for Na'vi River Journey before crowds arrive, then work your way through Africa for Kilimanjaro Safaris while animals are most active in the cooler morning air. Schedule the Festival of the Lion King or Finding Nemo show during midday when young kids need a seated rest, and use that time to enjoy a relaxed lunch at one of the park's family-friendly dining spots. Wrap up your afternoon with The Boneyard play area and any remaining character meet-and-greets, giving little ones a fun, low-key finish before heading back to your hotel for a well-earned early evening rest.
